# HYP-007: Taurine's Geroprotection Is Inflammasome-Mediated, Not Deficiency-Correction

**Domain:** Longevity / inflammaging
**Status:** OPEN for community peer review
**Generated:** 2026-11-11 by DeSci Lab (literature-sourced)

## The contradiction this resolves

The taurine–aging field currently contains a genuine, unresolved conflict:

1. **The deficiency model (2023).** A landmark *Science* study reported that circulating taurine *declines* with age across species and that supplementation extended median mouse lifespan ~10–12% while improving aging biomarkers in monkeys — framing taurine loss as a **driver** of aging to be corrected. [1]
2. **The rebuttal (2024–2026).** Subsequent cross-species analyses found that circulating taurine does **not** reliably decline with age in humans, mice, or monkeys — and actually *increases* in some cohorts (e.g., women) — undercutting the "restore a lost factor" premise. [2][4]
3. **The persistent effect (2026).** Despite #2, a 2026 *Cell* study found that supplementing *already-replete* older mice still reduced age-related inflammation by inhibiting the **NLRP3 inflammasome**, improving glucose tolerance, grip strength, and coordination. [3]

These only conflict if taurine's benefit depends on reversing a deficiency. They **reconcile** if the benefit is pharmacological, not restorative.

## Hypothesis

> Taurine's geroprotective effect is mediated primarily by **NLRP3 inflammasome suppression and downstream IL-1β attenuation**, and is therefore **independent of an individual's baseline taurine status**. The 2023 lifespan effect and the 2026 inflammation effect are the *same* mechanism; the "age-related decline" framing is incidental, not causal.

## Falsifiable predictions

- **P1 (decoupling):** In old animals stratified by baseline circulating taurine, healthspan/lifespan benefit will **not** correlate with baseline deficiency, but **will** correlate with the magnitude of NLRP3/IL-1β suppression after dosing.
- **P2 (mechanistic gate):** In *Nlrp3*-knockout aged mice, taurine supplementation will produce **little to no** additional inflammaging benefit (effect is routed through NLRP3).
- **P3 (dose–response on the inflammatory axis):** Benefit tracks achieved tissue taurine *exposure* and IL-1β reduction, not the gap between baseline and "young" levels.

## Why it matters

If true, trial design changes: stop selecting/segmenting participants by baseline taurine (a non-predictive biomarker per [2]) and instead power trials on inflammasome/IL-1β readouts. It reframes taurine from "replacement therapy" to a cheap, oral NLRP3-axis modulator — directly relevant to inflammaging across longevity.
